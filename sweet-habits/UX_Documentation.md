# 🌸 Sweet Habits — UX Research & Validation Plan

> This document separates **planned research** from **actual findings** so the portfolio never presents invented results as evidence.

---

## 🧁 Research Objective

Understand how users approach lightweight habit tracking and identify where setup, daily completion, feedback, and progress review create friction.

---

## 🍬 Research Questions

1. Why do people abandon habit trackers?
2. Which habit-setup options feel necessary vs. excessive?
3. Which feedback styles feel motivating, neutral, or stressful?
4. What information do users expect on a daily dashboard?
5. How do users recover after missing a day?
6. What progress information is useful weekly?

---

## 🔎 Competitive Audit

Review 3–5 existing products.

For each, capture:
- First-run experience
- Habit setup complexity
- Daily completion interaction
- Progress visualization
- Reminder model
- Tone of voice
- Accessibility observations
- Opportunity / risk

## 💬 Interview Plan

### Suggested sample
5–8 participants who currently use or have previously used habit / routine / productivity tools.

### Example prompts
- Tell me about the last habit tracker you tried.
- What made you keep using it or stop?
- What do you want to see first when you open one?
- How do streaks make you feel?
- What happens when you miss a day?
- Which reminders are useful vs. annoying?

Avoid leading participants toward the “cute/cozy” concept before learning what they actually value.

---

## 🧠 Synthesis

After interviews:
1. Pull notable statements.
2. Group observations by theme.
3. Separate behaviors from preferences.
4. Identify repeated pain points.
5. Turn patterns into design opportunities.

Do not manufacture persona demographics. If a persona is created, base it on observed patterns.

---

## 🗺️ Usability Test Plan

### Prototype
High-fidelity Figma or Framer prototype.

### Suggested participants
5–8 for the first qualitative round.

### Tasks
- Add a daily habit.
- Set a reminder.
- Complete today's habit.
- Find weekly progress.
- Edit the habit.

### Observe
- Whether the participant completes the task
- Where they hesitate
- Unexpected navigation
- Misunderstood labels
- Assistance required
- Accessibility/friction issues
- Comments made during or after the task

---

## 📊 Reporting Results

Use **counts and observations** that match the sample.

Good:
> “5 of 6 participants found weekly progress without assistance.”

Avoid unsupported claims:
> “92% of users loved the feature.”

For tiny qualitative samples, specific observations are usually more useful than impressive-looking percentages.

---

## ♿ Accessibility Review

Evaluate the design against relevant WCAG 2.2 AA considerations, including:
- Text contrast
- Non-text contrast
- Target sizing
- Focus appearance specifications
- Error identification
- Motion alternatives
- Non-color indicators
- Text resizing assumptions

The portfolio should say **“designed and evaluated against relevant WCAG 2.2 AA criteria”**, not claim certification or full compliance unless that is actually verified.
