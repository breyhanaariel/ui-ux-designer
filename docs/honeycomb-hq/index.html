🍯 Honeycomb HQ

A responsive operations workspace for small creative agencies managing clients, projects, approvals, deadlines, team workload, billing, and day-to-day risk.

Try Live PrototypeGitHub Case StudyFigma

Core product problem

The design question is not simply how to store agency information, but how to help a small team identify what needs attention now—risk, approvals, deadlines, capacity, and financial consequences.

Research status

Honest status: the repo documents hypotheses, roles, workflow questions, and a research/testing plan. It does not claim real agency interviews or usability results yet.

Information architecture

Primary navigation covers Overview, Projects, Clients, Approvals, Team, Billing, Activity, and Settings, with global search and notifications supporting cross-workflow awareness.

Core workflows

  • Resolve a project risk
  • Review/remind/record client approval
  • Create a project through a multi-step flow
  • Rebalance workload by reassigning work
  • Follow up on an overdue invoice and record activity

Dashboard & data density

The interface prioritizes actionability over decorative metrics. Table/search/filter states are designed to help users identify health, ownership, deadlines, approval status, and billing context.

Responsive strategy

Desktop emphasizes scanning and dense operational context. Mobile shifts toward prioritized actions and focused detail rather than simply shrinking the desktop table.

Working coded prototype

The prototype includes Overview, Projects, Clients, Approvals, Team, Billing, Activity, Settings, grouped search, notifications, filtering/sorting, project detail/editing, project creation, approval decisions/reminders, team reassignment, invoice follow-up, activity updates, and mobile navigation.

Accessibility & state handling

The coded experience includes keyboard controls, visible focus, form labels, modal focus handling, responsive transformations, non-color status communication, and multiple empty/error/permission/offline examples.

Testing & iteration

Planned usability tasks focus on identifying the highest-risk project, locating blockers and approvals, filtering by owner, reassigning work, finding overdue invoices, and creating projects. Evidence-driven iteration remains future work.

Next evidence to add

  • Competitive audit and interviews with relevant operators
  • Alternative dashboard wireframes and selection rationale
  • Formal usability findings and iteration stories
  • Broader high-fidelity Figma screen family and accessibility review
